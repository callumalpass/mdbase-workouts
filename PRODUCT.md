# Product: MDBase Workouts

register: product

## Product Purpose

MDBase Workouts is a personal workout log, planner, and history browser for someone who treats training as a long-running practice rather than a gamified challenge. It stores structured records as readable markdown files, using `mdbase` as the typed source of truth. The app should make daily training feel easy to enter, easy to revisit, and slightly unusual in its tactile, archival character.

## Audience

- Primary user: a self-directed lifter or generalist trainee who already has a training habit and wants a fast, private, durable log.
- Secondary user: a technically comfortable person who values local files, plain text, inspectable data, and tools that do not hide the record behind a service account.
- The user is not looking for social features, achievement badges, AI coaching, influencer-program polish, or a corporate wellness dashboard.

## Product Character

The app should feel simple, minimal, and bookish. It can be a little odd: more like a pocket training ledger, annotated field guide, or library index than a glossy fitness product. It should reward repeated use through calm density, legibility, and small moments of craft.

The app should not feel childish, macho, neon, sporty, social, or over-coached. Avoid the usual fitness-app tropes: heroic bodies, streak theatrics, challenge language, loud gradients, badges everywhere, and aggressively motivational copy.

## Strategic Principles

1. Logging beats presentation. The fastest path to capturing a workout matters more than decorative dashboards.
2. The record is the product. History, plain-file persistence, and chronology should feel trustworthy and inspectable.
3. Quiet is not bland. Use typographic texture, ruled lines, paper tone, labels, stamps, and measured rhythm to create character without clutter.
4. Plans are provisional. The interface should make it natural to schedule, start, adjust, and finish without treating deviation as failure.
5. Data should stay human. Sets, dates, exercises, PRs, and notes should read like entries in a training notebook, not analytics software.
6. Remove anything that asks for attention without helping the next workout or the long-term record.

## Core Workflows

- Open Today and immediately see what can be started, logged, or reviewed.
- Start from a template or plan, record sets with minimal friction, and finish with optional rating and notes.
- Add a one-off quick log without creating a full session.
- Browse a calendar for chronological context.
- Search history by session or exercise and inspect progression.
- Manage exercises, templates, settings, and local data paths without losing the sense of a small personal tool.

## Tone

Short, concrete, and unforced. Prefer labels like "Planned", "Completed", "Quick Logs", "Sets / week", and "History". Use restrained verbs: Start, Log, Save, Finish, Create, Edit. Avoid pep talks, jokes, exclamation marks, and copy that explains obvious UI behavior.

## Anti-References

- Strava-style social performance feeds.
- Apple Fitness rings and celebration-heavy streak mechanics.
- Corporate LMS or hospital-chart sterility.
- Neon dark-mode gym dashboards.
- Generic SaaS cards with icon grids and gradient metrics.
- Mascot-driven habit apps.

## Success Criteria

- The app is simpler after every major pass.
- The primary tab set remains small and obvious.
- A workout can be logged on mobile without reading instructions.
- Dense history remains scannable on small screens.
- Visual personality comes from typography, paper, lines, stamps, and spacing, not from decoration.
- The app feels worth keeping for years because the record feels durable.
